This weekend, I had the privilege of volunteering in the concession stand at the Stow Band Show! I figured that I could volunteer to help there since I don't have a kid playing in the show yet - not for a couple years. It was great to see how they run the concession stand, how we cheer for all the bands, and how excited the kids were about the band show. This band kid remembers that excitement.

I talked with some parents whose kids continued their music adventures in college, and some whose kids have not. I understand hoping that your kid carries the music forward. I also understand letting it go.

I didn't carry my music forward as much as I would've imagined. I didn't carry my clarinet through all of high school - it only showed up in my last year, for a semester in a music class. It also followed me to college only to make a semester appearance - playing a semester of orchestra in my sophomore year. I did eventually pick up a MIDI keyboard while in college, after I moved off-campus. It has since followed me to where I am today. My clarinet from the mid-to-late 1990s is also here.

I had dreams of playing clarinet in the Cleveland Orchestra. But then I showed some talents in the computer programming realm.  Deep down, as much as I wanted to go the music route, I knew that there's better money to be had in computer programming. With a better money outlook, that means that I could work the job that pays me enough to afford to buy my own instruments, eventually.

So I followed my computer programming route for my career. A few years into my career, I bought my 3/4-sized double bass. The more recent instrument purchase is my tenor recorder. Up next is probably a bass recorder - because [The Mandalorian score](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aQIcZbzr9Wk) got me.

Nowadays, I don't play as much. We had to move the MIDI keyboard out of my office to give me more room. My double bass and my clarinet live in a music corner in my room with a few other instruments - including my tin whistle and starting recorder. The only instrument in my home office currently is my tenor recorder - so that I can take some music breaks to help regulate myself.

Do I regret not pursuing my dream of playing in the Cleveland Orchestra? No regrets here. I work a job that enables me to take my family to their performances. I get to share my love of them with my husband and kids.

Is it still a dream worth pursuing? I think about this every time. I'm not Julliard trained. I didn't chase my dreams of training in a conservatory. There is no way I would pursue it in my current state. Now, would I go study at a conservatory? Maybe after the kids have moved on. Right now, I don't have time I can commit to studying and practicing music to that quality.

Do I have any closer goals musically? I'd love to bring my MIDI keyboard 🎹 back into the home office for better music breaks. The other ridiculous goal is to play the Law & Order theme on my clarinet. 🤣
